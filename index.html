the approach

More than tonight’s homework.

A lot of tutoring stops at tonight’s assignment. That holds up until the material starts building on itself, and in math and CS it always does.

I’d rather find the gap and fix it first, then put it to work on whatever is due. Once a student sees why a loop stops or why a proof holds, the next fifty problems stop being fifty separate things to memorize.

how it works

What a week looks like

Same rhythm every week, so nobody has to guess what comes next.

Getting started: a free 15-minute call, then a first session to see where things stand. After that, we settle into the weekly rhythm below.

  1. before

    The student sends notes

    They email me what they’re stuck on, whether it’s a problem set, a photo of their work, or a confusing slide, so I can prep ahead of time.

  2. during

    We meet, online or in person

    On Zoom with screen sharing, or in person with paper in parts of the Bay Area.

  3. after

    Notes go out by email

    A short recap of what we covered and what to practice, plus a PDF of everything I wrote, so the worked examples stay with them.

Then it repeats the following week. Or not, depending on your schedule!

questions

Before you book

What do you charge?

It depends on the course and how often we meet, so I’d rather give you a straight answer than a range that doesn’t fit. Bring it up on the 15-minute call and I’ll tell you exactly what it would cost.

How do payment and scheduling work?

Sessions are booked through the calendar below. I invoice by email, and Zelle or Venmo both work.

Cancellations with 24 hours’ notice get rescheduled at no charge.

Are sessions online or in person?

Both. Most families choose Zoom. In person depends on where you are in the Bay Area, so ask on the call.

How often should we meet?

Once a week works best for ongoing coursework. For one specific exam or a single rough unit, two or three sessions is often enough.

Do you help with homework directly?

We use the homework as the material, but I won’t hand over answers. If something’s due in an hour and nothing makes sense yet, that’s a conversation about planning, not a tutoring session.

My student isn’t struggling. They want to go further.

Great, those are some of my favorite students. Finished your school’s CS sequence and want to build something real, prep for a competition, or go deeper into algorithms? That works.
